What is UGC and how to use it.

UGC is one of the most useful and most misunderstood terms in creator marketing. Here is what it means, where it works and how to brief it properly.

By Karan Aryan, Founder, CreatorChahiye · Updated October 2026

In short

UGC (user-generated content) is content made by real customers about your product, which a brand reuses in its own ads, product pages and social channels. Brands also commission UGC-style content from creators to a brief. It is different from an influencer post, which is published to the creator's own audience, and paid or gifted content needs a clear disclosure label. Most UGC projects succeed or fail on the brief and the usage rights.

What UGC means

UGC stands for user-generated content. In the strict sense it is content that real customers create about a product on their own, without being paid or directed by the brand: a review, an unboxing, a photo of the product in use. Brands then ask permission to reuse it in their own ads, product pages and social channels, because people tend to trust what other users say more than brand copy.

In everyday practice, brands also commission "UGC-style" content from creators: authentic-looking videos made to a brief, which the brand uses on its own channels. The rest of this guide covers both, and the difference matters for disclosure, as explained below.

UGC vs influencer marketing

Where it is publishedYour brand's channels, ads and product pagesThe creator's own profile
What you are buyingThe content and the right to use itReach to the creator's audience, plus content
Creator follower countMatters lessMatters a lot
Key thing to agreeUsage rights: where and for how longDeliverables and posting schedule

Where brands use UGC

  • Paid social ads, where authentic-looking creative often fits the feed better than polished studio content
  • Product pages and landing pages
  • Organic social posts and Stories
  • Email campaigns
  • Marketplace listings

Common UGC formats

  • Unboxing and first impressions
  • Product demo or "how I use it"
  • Honest review or testimonial
  • Before-and-after or routine videos
  • Short talking-head videos answering one common question

How to brief UGC creators

A short, clear brief gets better content than a long vague one. Include:

  1. The product and who it is for
  2. The one main message
  3. Format, length and orientation, for example a 20 to 30 second vertical video
  4. What to show and what to avoid
  5. Deadline and number of revision rounds
  6. Usage rights: where the content can be used and for how long

Usage rights, in plain words

Whether content is customer-made or commissioned, you need permission to reuse it. Usage rights decide what you are allowed to do with the content. Can you run it as a paid ad? On your website? For how long? These terms change the price, so agree them in writing before the creator starts. This is one reason every CreatorChahiye campaign runs on a written agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Is UGC the same as influencer marketing?

No. UGC is content made for a brand to use in its own channels. Influencer marketing publishes content to a creator's own audience. They can be combined in one campaign.

Do UGC creators need a large following?

Not necessarily. Because the content is used on your channels, the quality of the video and how natural it feels often matter more than follower count.

Who owns UGC content?

It depends on the agreement. Typically the creator owns the content and the brand receives usage rights for agreed channels and a set period. Put the terms in writing.

How many creators should I start with?

Start small. Working with a few creators lets you test different styles and see what performs before you scale.

How to choose micro-influencers for your brand.

Follower count is the least useful number on a creator's profile. Here is the checklist we use to find creators whose audience actually fits.

By Karan Aryan, Founder, CreatorChahiye · Updated October 2026

In short

Choose micro-influencers on audience fit, not follower count. Check who follows them (city, age, interests), whether their engagement looks genuine, whether their content style suits your product, and test with a small paid pilot before scaling.

In this guide

  1. Start with your customer, not the creator
  2. The 6-step selection checklist

Start with your customer, not the creator

Before you look at any profile, write down who you want to reach: city, age range, interests, language. A creator with 20,000 followers in your target city can outperform one with 200,000 followers spread across the country. This is why we match on audience fit first and follower count last.

The 6-step selection checklist

  1. Shortlist by niche, city and language. Look for creators already posting about topics close to your product.
  2. Ask for audience insights. Creators can share screenshots from Instagram's professional dashboard showing top cities, age range and gender split. Compare these against your customer.
  3. Check engagement quality. Read the comments. Do they respond to the content, or are they generic emojis and one-word replies?
  4. Compare views to followers. Look at average Reel views against follower count. A very large gap is worth asking about.
  5. Review past sponsored posts. Do those posts get a similar response to the creator's regular content?
  6. Test before you scale. Run a small paid pilot with a few creators and compare results.

Red flags to watch for

  • Sudden jumps in followers with no matching jump in views or comments
  • Comments that look copy-pasted or unrelated to the post
  • Reluctance to share audience insights
  • Audience concentrated in countries or cities you don't sell to
  • A feed that is almost entirely sponsored content
  • Anyone asking you for money to "get listed" or to start working with you

Nano, micro or macro?

TierGood forWatch out for
Nano (1K–10K)Authentic, local, low-cost content; product seedingNeeds many creators for reach; more coordination
Micro (10K–50K)Balance of reach and trust; the sweet spot for most startupsQuality varies a lot, so vet carefully
Mid-micro (50K–200K)More reach while staying fairly targetedCost rises fast here; confirm the audience fits before committing

Lock it in writing

Once you've picked creators, agree deliverables, deadlines, payment terms and usage rights in writing. It protects your budget, and it protects the creator's payment too. Every CreatorChahiye campaign runs on a written agreement for exactly this reason.

Frequently asked questions

What is a micro-influencer?

A micro-influencer is generally a creator with a mid-sized, focused audience, often described as roughly 10,000 to 100,000 followers. Definitions vary, and many brands also work with nano creators below 10,000.

How do I check if an influencer has fake followers?

Look for follower spikes with no matching engagement, generic repetitive comments, and large gaps between followers and average views. Ask for audience insights screenshots and compare them with the creator's claimed location.

Is engagement rate more important than followers?

Both matter, but engagement quality and audience fit matter more than raw follower count. A smaller, engaged audience in the right city is usually more valuable than a large mismatched one.

How many creators should I start with?

For a first campaign, a pilot of around 5 to 10 nano and micro creators is a sensible start. It gives you enough variety to see what works without a large commitment.

Nano vs micro influencers: which should you pick?

Both tiers beat big names on engagement per rupee for many brands. The real question is how much reach, how much effort and how much risk you want.

By Karan Aryan, Founder, CreatorChahiye · Updated October 2026

In short

Nano influencers (roughly 1,000 to 10,000 followers) usually have the highest engagement and the lowest cost, but you need many of them for reach. Micro influencers (roughly 10,000 to 100,000) offer a balance of reach and trust. Tier definitions vary by source, so judge by audience fit, not just the label.

The tiers in plain numbers

Definitions vary between platforms and reports. The ranges below are commonly used, and our other guides use slightly different bands, so treat them as a guide rather than a rule.

TierTypical followers
Nano1,000 to 10,000
Micro10,000 to 100,000
Macro100,000 to 1,000,000
Mega or celebrity1,000,000 and above

What the benchmarks say

Benchmark reports consistently show engagement rates falling as follower counts rise. In the Influencer Marketing Hub benchmark report on Instagram, nano influencers averaged about 6.2% engagement and micro influencers about 3.9%. Other sources report similar patterns. These are global averages and vary by niche, platform and how engagement is calculated, so use them as a rough comparison, not a promise.

How to choose

  1. Start with your goal. Awareness across a wide area needs more reach; local trust or product testing suits nano.
  2. Look at budget and time. Managing 20 nano creators takes more effort than managing 5 micro creators.
  3. Check audience fit first. A well-matched small creator beats a mismatched larger one.
  4. Mix the tiers. Many brands run a few micro creators for reach and a group of nano creators for authentic content.
  5. Test and compare. Run a small pilot and compare cost per result.

For a first campaign, our startup playbook suggests a small pilot of nano and micro creators.

What about cost?

Rates depend on tier, format, usage rights and exclusivity, not just follower count. See our guide to influencer marketing costs in India for how to plan the budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between nano and micro influencers?

Mainly audience size. Nano influencers are commonly defined as having about 1,000 to 10,000 followers, and micro influencers about 10,000 to 100,000, though definitions vary.

Do nano influencers have better engagement?

Benchmark reports usually show higher engagement rates for smaller accounts. For example, the Influencer Marketing Hub Instagram benchmark reported about 6.2% for nano and 3.9% for micro influencers. Results vary by niche.

Which is better for a startup, nano or micro?

Many startups start with a mix: a few micro creators for reach and several nano creators for authentic content, then scale what works.

Is a bigger following always better?

No. Audience fit and genuine engagement matter more than follower count.

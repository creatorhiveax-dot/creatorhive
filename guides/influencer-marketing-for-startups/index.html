Influencer marketing for startups: where to start.

You don't need a big budget or an in-house team. You need one clear goal, a small pilot and a way to measure it.

By Karan Aryan, Founder, CreatorChahiye · Updated October 2026

In short

Start with one goal, a small pilot of 5–10 nano and micro creators, a written brief, trackable links or codes, and a two-to-four-week review window. Learn from the pilot, then scale what worked.

Step 1: pick one goal

Decide whether this campaign is for awareness, app installs, website traffic or sales. Trying to achieve all four with a small budget usually achieves none. Your goal decides the creators you pick, the content format and what you measure.

Step 2: design a small pilot

Work with a handful of nano and micro creators rather than one big name. A pilot gives you several creatives and audiences to compare, so you learn faster and risk less. If you're unsure how to split the budget, see our cost guide, and pick creators using the micro-influencer checklist.

Step 3: write a clear brief

  • What the product is and who it's for
  • The one message you want people to remember
  • Must-include points and things to avoid
  • Format (for example one Reel plus Stories), deadline and approval process
  • Hashtags, tags and how to use Instagram's Collab feature

Give creators room to speak in their own voice. Content that feels scripted tends to perform worse than content that sounds like the creator.

Step 4: set up tracking before you launch

Give each creator a unique link with UTM parameters or a unique promo code, so you can see which creators drive visits and sales. Without this, you only see likes and views, which don't tell you if the campaign worked.

Step 5: agree everything in writing

A one-page agreement covering deliverables, timeline, payment terms, usage rights and approval process saves arguments later. It also makes creators more comfortable working with a new brand.

Step 6: review, learn, scale

After two to four weeks, compare creators on clicks, sign-ups or sales, not just views. Double down on the types of creators and content that worked, and drop what didn't.

Common mistakes startups make

  • Choosing creators by follower count alone
  • Spending the whole budget on one creator
  • Launching without any tracking
  • Skipping a written agreement
  • Judging the campaign after three days

Creator-led storytelling also works well for startups. For example, we're producing creator-led startup stories across the T-Hub ecosystem, where the story comes first and the product second.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a startup spend on its first influencer campaign?

Many startups begin with roughly ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 in creator fees for a pilot across several nano and micro creators. The right number depends on your goal and category.

How long does an influencer campaign take to show results?

Content usually goes live within one to two weeks of briefing, and early signals appear in the first days after posting. Judge results over two to four weeks, not days.

Should startups work with an agency or directly with creators?

You can do either. Working directly is cheaper but takes time for sourcing, negotiation and follow-up. An agency saves that time and adds vetting, at the cost of a management fee.

Which platform should a startup start with?

For most consumer brands in India, Instagram Reels is a sensible first channel. Add YouTube if your product needs longer explanations.
